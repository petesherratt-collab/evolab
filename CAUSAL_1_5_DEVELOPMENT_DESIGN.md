## Public interpretation contract

## Acquisition schedule

Keep two tool-enabled rounds per episode, the 18-request condition ceiling,
25,000–30,000-token envelope, 1,800 completion cap and all evidence allocations.
Tell each caller its remaining tool rounds, including the current one, and that
a tool request can contain multiple evidence IDs. The last tool round explicitly
asks for affordable prerequisite bundles instead of unexecutable sequential plans.
Private reviews still cannot purchase evidence.

The interpretation registry makes the active bundle identifiable after the
first routing purchase. Where the full agent/collective budget permits, the
remaining route can be requested in the second round. Independent investigators
with smaller individual budgets must choose affordable subsets; they cannot
pool budgets or access other investigators' observations. No forced or oracle-
selected purchase is added to the live harness.

A scripted offline client must demonstrate registry-then-batch acquisition in
the actual two-round executor. This proves schedule feasibility for that fixture,
not that a live model will follow the instruction. If live models still request
unexecutable plans, a later separately specified scheduling change may be needed.

## Reasoning and observability

Replace the blanket prohibition on marginal intersections with a narrower
distinction: applying disclosed XOR, calibration and compatibility rules is
permitted after their stated prerequisites are resolved; treating correlated
probabilities as independent remains unwarranted. The instruction applies to
all conditions. Preserve evidence-based revisions and residual uncertainty.

Retain v1.4 scout handoff, one bounded recovery attempt, future-participant
reserves and existing token/cost accounting. Add ledger events for tools offered,
raw requested tool calls, successful and denied tool results, and whether a call
returned voluntarily, exhausted tool rounds or used a compute fallback. Tool
results are recorded when passed to the next model invocation; all successful
purchases also retain the original immediate acquisition events. A process
interruption between tool execution and the next invocation could therefore
leave detailed denial events absent, while successful purchases remain recorded.
